The stakes are also different. You're often handling client data, internal systems, or financial information from networks you don't control and have no way to vet. Reliability and consistent performance become the priority, more than any single flashy feature.

What matters most for work travel

  • Connection stability. A VPN that drops mid-call defeats the purpose.
  • Fast server switching. Conference WiFi and hotel networks vary wildly in quality — being able to quickly try another server matters.
  • Strong encryption as a baseline. Non-negotiable when client or company data is involved.

Before your next work trip

  1. Test the VPN on your laptop before you travel, not for the first time in a lobby five minutes before a call.
  2. Know how to quickly switch servers or protocols in case one connection type is blocked on a given network.
  3. Keep your VPN app updated — connection protocols occasionally change and older versions can be less reliable.

Frequently asked questions

Do I still need a personal VPN if my company provides one?

A corporate VPN typically secures access to company systems specifically, while a personal VPN protects your broader connection on public networks — many business travelers use both.

Will a VPN cause issues on hotel business-center or conference WiFi?

Occasionally a locked-down network restricts VPN traffic; switching server or protocol within the app usually resolves it.
